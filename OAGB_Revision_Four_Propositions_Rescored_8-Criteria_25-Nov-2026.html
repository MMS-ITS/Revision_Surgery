Twelve claims in the earlier reports that do not hold up

  1. "Permanently shuts off acid via Truncal Vagotomy" and "carries zero risk of 'post-vagotomy diarrhoea' because your bypass anatomy has no pyloric valve to stagnate". Gemini report; dashboard
    A vagotomy lowers acid; it does not shut it off. It is usually less complete than the vonoprazan that had already failed to heal the ulcer. Your remnant stomach keeps its pylorus. Post-vagotomy bowel upset also comes from denervating the gut and the biliary tree, not only from drainage procedures. Published salvage series report dysphagia and aspiration after vagotomy, and they report diarrhoea too sparsely to support "zero".

    Salvage vagotomy for marginal ulcer is commonly done thoracoscopically, through the chest, well away from a repaired hiatus. The 2019 comparison and later robotic series used this route. Keeping vagotomy in reserve is a realistic option.

Two questions behind the scores

How much bowel would food travel without bile? (bile limb + food limb, cm)
Ranges follow each surgeon's written figures. Dr Usama and Dr Üstün gave none, so two scenarios are shown. The dashed line marks today's 155 cm. The 2024 expert consensus asks surgeons to measure the whole bowel and keep at least 300 to 400 cm of common channel.

What a vagotomy can and cannot do here

  • Can: lower acid made by the pouch for life, and so reduce reliance on tablets. It is technically easy during a hiatal hernia repair.
  • Cannot: remove bile or H. pylori, or heal a scarred ulcer bed on its own. It is weaker than vonoprazan, which did not heal this ulcer in 12 weeks.
  • Cost: it is permanent. There are few data on long-term bowel effects in bypass patients. Highly selective vagotomy is unsafe here, as Prof. Lee rightly says, because the pouch depends on lesser-curve blood vessels.
  • Fair reading: neither "obsolete" nor "most important". It is a reasonable reserve step, which can be done later through the chest if an ulcer returns despite PPI and a confirmed H. pylori cure.

Keep the ulcerated join, or cut it out?

  • Keeping it (Prof. Lee) avoids the step most prone to leaks and narrowing, and makes the operation shorter. But the ulcer that bled stays in place, unsampled, until it heals, and nobody will check it unless you ask.
  • Removing it (Dr Usama, Dr Fakhrul) takes away the bleeding source at once and gives the pathologist tissue. It allows a fresh, well-supplied join and, if the surgeon chooses, a shorter bile limb. The price is a new join in revisional tissue.
  • What would tip it: a fresh endoscopy. If the ulcer has healed on dexlansoprazole and sucralfate and the join looks healthy, keeping it becomes more defensible. If it is still deep or scarred, removal is the clearer choice.

Method, sources and limits

How the scores were made

  • Each proposition was scored 0 to 10 on each of your eight criteria and weighted as you set them (25, 10, 20, 15, 10, 10, 7 and 3%).
  • A score rates the written proposition, with any surgeon-specific facts in the emails. Self-reported results were given limited weight because none is audited.
  • "Not stated" was scored as a gap, not as a failure. A call may have covered it, but nothing in writing shows that.
  • Dr Fakhrul's design was scored as a proposition. His lack of an operating team is reflected in operative safety and completeness. A "bookable only" ranking puts Dr Usama first, Dr Üstün second and Prof. Lee third.
  • Criterion 8 was checked against public profiles on 25 November 2026. Prof. Lee is a past president of the IFSO Asia-Pacific Chapter. Dr Üstün practises in Istanbul and London. Dr Fakhrul's credentials are as stated in his file. No public profile of Dr Usama's training was found, so his score rests on the emails.

Limits

  • Endoscopy images, operative findings and pathology were not available.
  • The literature is small and retrospective. No study compares these four operations in a patient like you.
  • Literature figures are taken from the cited papers' abstracts, as also used in the earlier reports. They were spot-checked, not fully re-read.
  • Records to reconcile: the second iron infusion is dated 17 October in one email and 25 October in another. The dexlansoprazole dose is described differently in different letters.
  • A difference of 0.3 points or less should not decide anything.
