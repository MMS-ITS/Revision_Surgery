Key trade-offs—not a simple “more surgery is better” rule

  • Resect/reconstruct (Iqbal; possibly Üstün): removes the ulcerated anastomosis and yields tissue for pathology, but entails a new GJ and additional dissection/anastomotic risk.
  • Preserve/reinforce (Lee): avoids a new GJ and may reduce some technical hazards, but leaves the lesion in place; durable healing, malignancy exclusion, and rescue plan need a clear answer.
  • Short limbs (earlier Islam proposal): may be nutrition-conscious at BMI about 26–27 and iron deficiency, but does not itself settle ulcer excision, hernia repair, or bile diversion reliability.
  • RYGB: separates the alimentary path from bile flow in principle, but the actual Roux limb, total bowel length/common channel and anatomy must be specified. No limb configuration guarantees reflux control.
  • H. pylori: Iqbal’s “eradicate if histopathology positive” does not replace a safe, explicit test-of-cure strategy after two antibiotic courses. Lee deprioritizes eradication; Üstün emphasizes specialist confirmation and safe testing.

Written questions to resolve before choosing or consenting

Ask each surgeon to answer for this anatomy and current bleeding/iron history—not in general terms.

  1. Ulcer and re-bleeding: Will the ulcerated GJ be resected, revised, or retained? Why is that preferable here? Will tissue be sent for histopathology? If retained, what is the objective healing endpoint, scheduled endoscopy timing, and rescue procedure if it persists or bleeds?
  2. H. pylori: What exact regimen was taken, and how will eradication be confirmed after prior antibiotic exposure? How can testing be done without unsafe interruption of PPI after bleeding on withdrawal? Who owns the plan with gastroenterology?
  3. Acid suppression: What perioperative and post-operative PPI dose/duration is planned? Why would treatment stop, and what are the criteria? What is the plan if symptoms or ulcer persist?
  4. Exact reconstruction: Give proposed BP limb, alimentary/Roux limb, common channel or total small-bowel length, measurement method, and intra-operative adjustment rules. How does the plan balance bile diversion, iron absorption, protein/calorie nutrition and current BMI?
  5. Iron deficiency: What are current CBC, ferritin, transferrin saturation and iron plan before surgery? Which iron/B12/folate/vitamin D/calcium/protein tests and replacement schedule will be used afterward, and how often? Is IV iron likely to be needed?
  6. Hernia / gallbladder: How will Hill IV hiatal hernia be confirmed and repaired? Will stones be removed during the same operation, and what is the rationale given the bypass anatomy and future biliary access?
  7. Safety: What are the surgeon’s audited denominator and follow-up for this exact or closest operation: leak, bleeding, reoperation, readmission, mortality and conversion-to-open? What are the specific contingencies for poor tissue, ischemia, adhesions or unexpected anatomy?
  8. Other operative details: Which mesenteric defects will be closed and how? Is a drain planned, and how will leak be detected if not? What thrombosis prevention, mobilization and international-travel/fit-to-fly plan is used?
  9. Readiness: Is surgery elective now or should anemia/ulcer/H. pylori be stabilized first? What anaesthesia assessment is needed for hypertension, bradycardia, hypersomnia and methylphenidate/modafinil? Reconcile medication lists and recent colon-polyp follow-up.
  10. Written consent plan: Can the surgeon provide the intended operation, alternatives, material risks, possible intra-operative changes, medication, follow-up and cost/complication coverage in a single written summary?
